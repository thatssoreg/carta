# Run 06 — Béarn / Jurançon generalization assessment

## What transferred cleanly?

The five-pillar reading model, progressive disclosure, map reactions, producer cards, evidence projection, glossary terms, trail history, explicit Explore-versus-Go behavior, and three considered next moves all transferred without changing the Jura content model. Jura and Béarn now opt into the same `regional_world` contract.

## What needed regional adaptation?

Béarn needed a different thesis and information hierarchy. Its opening is the relationship between wet Atlantic foothills, a drying autumn foehn, late local grapes, retained acidity, and three legal Jurançon paths. Percentage grape-share cards did not fit, so the shared grape-card component now accepts role labels such as principal, accessory, and wider-region context. The Rules pillar also accepts data-authored groups instead of assuming Jura's four-origin/two-category structure.

## What proved Jura-specific rather than Atlas doctrine?

Jura's six-AOP overlap pattern, claimed-area comparison, ouillé/sous-voile fork, and lineage/access lenses remain Jura content. They are no longer encoded as the only way a finished regional world can work. Likewise, ordinary map-click precedence is now release data: it can prefer geographic Jura AOPs over Crémant/Macvin and Jurançon over the overlapping Béarn layer without a region-name conditional.

## What new reusable need emerged?

The Atlas needed four reusable capabilities: layered release overlays, a multi-region producer-point source, regional role labels that are not percentages, and reciprocal context returns that preserve both subject history and map viewport. Run 06 adds all four while retaining Run 05 as the base and regression case.

## Evidence boundaries retained

- The 620-hectare figure remains explicitly scoped to association-member vineyards, not total Jurançon AOP area.
- Producer markers represent published production bases or disclosed approximate localities, never vineyard parcels or fruit sources.
- Petit Manseng's sugar capacity is not presented as a sweetness guarantee.
- Petit Manseng and Gros Manseng remain separate cultivars; Courbu and Petit Courbu remain separate names.
- Raffiat de Moncade is shown as wider Béarn/Pyrenean context, not as a current Jurançon-permitted grape.
- Imanol Garay and Alfredo Egia appear as cross-border cultural context, not as silently relabeled Jurançon growers.
- No sensory Tell was invented for Béarn/Jurançon.

## Deferred research dossiers

1. A genuinely sourceable bottle-level sensory Tell for Jurançon, if one can be established without turning a producer or vintage into a universal regional shortcut.
2. First-party standalone dossiers for Clos Uroulat and Domaine de Souch beyond the bounded address, range, scale, grape, and farming claims used here.
3. Parcel- or vineyard-scale geometry only where authoritative evidence can distinguish estate holdings, leased land, and purchased fruit.
