# Run 03G Deep Research Reconciliation Audit

## Outcome

Run 03G **completed all four requested dossiers**, so the one-time four-seed completion exception succeeded structurally.

Factually, however, the report's self-certification is unreliable. Three of the four dossiers contain major identity errors and the fourth contains important geography, ownership and chronology errors.

**Disposition:** independently rebuild the four cellar worlds from source-addressable first-party, regulatory and high-fitness specialist evidence; ingest only the recovered core; preserve the research report as discovery substrate rather than authority.

This closes the original 23-seed cellar expansion after reconciliation.

## Structural success

The report did what the failed six-seed Run 03F did not:

- all four dossiers were present;
- no dossier was left as a placeholder;
- the final sections were complete enough to inspect;
- the report surfaced some uncertainty rather than suppressing every gap.

That means four seeds can fit in the output envelope when prose is controlled.

It does **not** mean four seeds should replace the three-seed default. Source integrity degraded materially.

## Major reconciliation findings

### 1. Alenza was assigned to the wrong winery

Run 03G says Alenza Gran Reserva is made by **Finca Villacreces** and places Villacreces inside the Alejandro Fernández / Grupo Pesquera family structure.

Current first-party Familia Fernández Rivera evidence instead identifies:

- winery: **Condado de Haza**;
- wine: **Alenza Gran Reserva**;
- 2019 grape: **100% Tempranillo**;
- vineyard: **Maricalva**;
- municipal source: **Roa and La Horra**;
- roughly 800 m;
- 27 months in **American oak**.

The current Familia Fernández Rivera portfolio lists Tinto Pesquera, Condado de Haza, Dehesa La Granja and El Vínculo. Finca Villacreces is not part of that current first-party portfolio.

Run 03G's Villacreces identity chain, Cabernet Sauvignon blend, estate history and related cellar material were rejected.

### 2. Scar of the Sea / Bassi was recognizable but materially distorted

Run 03G gets the producer and wine family broadly right, but important details are wrong.

Current first-party evidence says:

- Scar of the Sea was established in **2012**;
- it is owned and operated by **Mikey and Gina Giugni**;
- they began fully farming Bassi in March 2024 and purchased it in July 2024;
- Bassi is in **Avila Valley**, about 1.2 miles from Avila Beach;
- sandstone is the dominant first-party soil descriptor;
- Bassi received **Demeter certification in July 2024**.

Independent reporting says Mikey and Gina are majority owners with Will and Coral Frischkorn as partners, and that Scar had purchased Bassi fruit since 2019.

TTB established **San Luis Obispo Coast / SLO Coast in 2022**, not 2014.

The report's placement of Bassi in Edna Valley, Bassi-family ownership narrative, speculative family relationship, new-oak assumptions and generic Chardonnay cellar inference were not accepted.

### 3. The report missed a real Phelan Farm connection

Run 03G says Scar of the Sea and Phelan Farm share only geography.

That is too narrow.

Phelan Farm's own site lists **Brij Bassi Syrah 2021** among wines from purchased grapes. In 2024, the San Francisco Chronicle also reported that Raj Parr agreed to provide Pink Chardonnay cuttings from Phelan Farm for Bassi replanting.

CARTA records this as a supported research lead, not an automatic propagation or collaboration edge. Brij identity and the actual material transfer still require direct resolution.

### 4. Ashes & Diamonds was built around a false founder and a false durable wine identity

Run 03G names **Andrew Marston** as founder/owner/winemaker.

Ashes & Diamonds identifies **Kashy Khaledi** as proprietor and owner and says he launched the winery in 2017.

The producer identifies **Steve Matthiasson** as the winemaker attached to the Cabernet Franc line.

The exact seed is **No 3 Cabernet Franc 2016**, not a likely 2020 release. Decanter records it as 80% Cabernet Franc and 20% Merlot at 13.5% ABV.

More importantly, producer pages show that the number changes:

- №6 in 2019;
- №10 in 2023.

Therefore CARTA models one durable `Ashes & Diamonds Cabernet Franc` wine identity and treats `No 3` as a 2016 label/release name.

The report's nearly-100%-Cabernet-Franc assumption, 30–36 months in 100% new French oak, Andrew Marston identity and invented López de Heredia/Rioja history were rejected.

### 5. Guillaume Gilles / Les Peyrouses was almost inverted

Run 03G says Les Peyrouses is a granite hillside outside Cornas and that a Robert Michel mentorship could not be documented.

High-fitness specialist sources establish almost the opposite:

- Les Peyrouses Rouge is **Côtes du Rhône**;
- it is **Syrah**;
- it comes from an old-vine parcel **on the flats in Cornas**;
- soils are sand and clay with large galets;
- Robert Michel explicitly **mentored** Gilles and is described as his protégé relationship;
- Gilles worked with Michel from 2000 through 2006, then spent time with Jean-Louis Chave before establishing independently.

INAO defines Cornas AOP as a delimited Syrah-only red appellation on the sole commune of Cornas. Physical location in the commune does not automatically mean a parcel is inside the AOP delimitation.

This is a useful STRATA example: **commune, parcel, appellation boundary and bottle designation are separate truths.**

## Self-audit failures

The Run 03G integrity section says no unresolved identities remain and no meaningful contamination survives. That assessment is false.

Examples include:

- wrong Alenza producer identity;
- wrong Ashes & Diamonds founder;
- wrong Ashes release chronology;
- wrong Les Peyrouses geography and relationship history;
- unrelated **Phaunus** material left in the Source Ledger;
- multiple speculative phrases such as “likely”, “presumably” and “rumored” presented alongside clean-audit language;
- generic or implausible source URLs used as if they were claim-bearing authority.

The report's completion audit was useful. Its factual/source audit was not.

## Four-seed lesson

The four-seed exception achieved completion, but source discipline deteriorated compared with 03F-A and 03F-B.

**Protocol conclusion remains: three seeds is the normal CARTA research envelope.**

When a batch is larger, prose can be compressed, but identity resolution and direct-source verification must not be allowed to thin out.
