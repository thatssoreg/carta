# Landscapes

Landscapes are physical, climatic, geological, hydrological, island, mountain, or cultural-geographic reference objects that help explain wine without being forced into national or appellation hierarchies.

A landscape can cross political borders.

That does **not** make it an ecosystem.

Examples of future landscape profiles may include the Western Pyrenees, river systems, alpine arcs, volcanic island groups, coastal corridors, or other geographies whose physical continuity matters to viticulture and wine culture.

## Landscape versus ecosystem

A landscape is fundamentally geographic.

An ecosystem is a relationship-generated analytical object and may contain connections that are geographically distant. The Pyrenean Atlantic ecosystem, for example, reaches Richard Leroy in the Loire through a professional network. That professional edge does not make the Loire part of the Western Pyrenees.
