# Gang of Four (Beaujolais)

**Morgon / Beaujolais, France**

CARTA models the **Gang of Four** as an informal **community of practice / historical ecosystem**, not as a formal organization.

Kermit Lynch identifies the four producers as:

- Marcel Lapierre
- Jean Foillard
- Guy Breton
- Jean-Paul Thévenet

and describes them as friends and followers of Jules Chauvet's methods. Lynch coined the `Gang of Four` nickname.

## Why this belongs in CARTA

This grouping is not merely a colorful merchant nickname. In Kermit Lynch's account, the producers' adoption of Chauvet-associated viticultural and cellar methods helped drive a dramatic improvement in Beaujolais wine quality and increased attention to Beaujolais crus, villages and regional wines.

That makes the cluster historically useful for CARTA's **Relationships** and **Time** dimensions: it helps explain transmission of practice and a meaningful change in the region's modern wine culture.

## Ontology treatment

CARTA represents the cluster as:

- `ecosystem:gang-of-four-beaujolais`
- four `MEMBER_OF` relationships whose notes explicitly define membership as informal community-of-practice membership
- Jules Chauvet as a contextual transmission figure already present in CARTA

CARTA does **not** infer that:

- the Gang of Four was incorporated or formally constituted;
- every pair of producers collaborated directly;
- every producer had the same exact viticultural or cellar protocol;
- Kermit Lynch's nickname exhausts the wider history of Beaujolais natural wine.

Direct bilateral mentorship, collaboration, employment, friendship or influence edges still require their own evidence.
