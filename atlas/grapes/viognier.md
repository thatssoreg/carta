# Viognier

**A grape that survives in law long before it recovers in the vineyard**

Viognier's legal position in the Northern Rhône is unusually stark. It is the sole variety of [Condrieu](../countries/france/appellations/condrieu.md) and of [Château-Grillet](../countries/france/appellations/chateau-grillet.md) — both specifications say only that the wines come from Viognier B — and it is the one white grape a [Côte-Rôtie](../countries/france/appellations/cote-rotie.md) red may contain, as an accessory variety planted among the Syrah and [co-fermented](../practices/co-fermentation.md) with it. It appears in the rules of none of the other five crus.

## Nearly gone

The Condrieu specification records phylloxera in 1893, the First World War, and industrial employment drawing labour off the terraces, until by 1965 some eight hectares remained. The variety's later international spread begins from that near-disappearance, not from abundance.

The Syndicat des Vignerons de l'AOC Condrieu credits [Georges Vernay](../producers/domaine-georges-vernay.md) with working to rebuild the hillsides and rescue the variety from obscurity. CARTA carries that as a documented leading role inside a longer collective recovery, not as sole causation.

## What CARTA does not yet hold

Parentage, synonymy and the international diaspora all wait for primary ampelographic authority. Viognier's story here is a legal and historical one; the genetics are a separate, unstarted pass.
