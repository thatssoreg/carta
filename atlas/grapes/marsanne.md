# Marsanne

**The reason the Northern Rhône is not only red**

Marsanne is the corridor's principal white variety. It is named with [Roussanne](roussanne.md) in the white rules of [Saint-Joseph](../countries/france/appellations/saint-joseph.md), [Crozes-Hermitage](../countries/france/appellations/crozes-hermitage.md), [Hermitage](../countries/france/appellations/hermitage.md) and [Saint-Péray](../countries/france/appellations/saint-peray.md); it is an accessory variety in the reds of the first three, admitted in mixed plantings and [assembled at the grape stage](../practices/co-fermentation.md); and it carries two wine forms that exist nowhere else in the corridor.

## Two forms most regions do not have

The [Hermitage vin de paille mention](../classifications/hermitage-vin-de-paille.md) is a white wine from grapes dried at least 45 days. Saint-Péray's *vins blancs mousseux* are made by [second fermentation in bottle](../practices/traditional-method-sparkling.md); [Domaine Alain Voge](../producers/domaine-alain-voge.md)'s Les Bulles d'Alain is 100% Marsanne, brut nature, four years on lattes — and the same estate's Ongrie, created in 1984, is 100% Marsanne vinified only in oak.

## Named in pairs, planted unequally

The Saint-Joseph specification notes Marsanne at about 95% of that appellation's white plantings. Legal parity with Roussanne is not the same as equal planting, and CARTA does not read one from the other.

Read the grape through [Northern Rhône](../countries/france/regions/northern-rhone.md).
