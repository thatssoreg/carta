# Gamay noir à jus blanc

**A grape identity is not a regional style recipe**

Gamay noir à jus blanc—written **Gamay N** in the current Beaujolais specification—is the principal red and rosé variety in broad Beaujolais and the central grape of the ten crus. The official 2024 regional snapshot reports Gamay at 96% of the regional grape distribution, but this measure does not make every wine, origin or cellar method the same.

## Parentage

Peer-reviewed microsatellite work supports Gamay as an offspring of **Pinot** and **Gouais blanc**. Chardonnay is another offspring of the same pair. Shared parentage is a biological relationship; it is not evidence of shared wine style, legal status or regional identity.

## The 1395 ordinance

Philip the Bold’s 1395 ordinance against Gamay in ducal Burgundy is a real historical event. CARTA keeps its historical territory separate from modern Burgundy geometry and rejects the tidy story that one decree documents a one-step migration into Beaujolais. The event is useful for understanding regulation and vineyard economy, not for manufacturing a modern border myth.

## Gamay and Beaujolais

The August 2026 Beaujolais specification permits Gamay N as the principal variety for red and rosé, alongside limited accessory varieties. White Beaujolais is Chardonnay. The ten cru identities remain distinct legal origins, even where Gamay is shared.

## Not a cellar instruction

Gamay does not require carbonic maceration. Carbonic, semi-carbonic and whole-cluster fermentation are separate concepts, and the current Beaujolais law does not impose one universal protocol. Producer-level claims remain attached to their producer and date.
