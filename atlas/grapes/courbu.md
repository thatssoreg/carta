# Courbu

Courbu is an old white Pyrenean grape associated with southwest France and, under the official Spanish designation **Hondarrabi Zuri**, the Basque wine world. It is distinct from Petit Courbu despite the confusingly similar names.

## At a glance

Plantgrape describes Courbu as:

- autochthonous to Pyrenean vineyards
- vigorous enough to require considered pruning
- sensitive to wind
- susceptible to grey rot
- small-berried
- capable of sugar-rich musts with moderate acidity
- able to produce fine wines

That combination gives Courbu a different structural role from the more acid-driven Mansengs.

## In the vineyard

Courbu's susceptibility to wind and grey rot makes site and canopy management important in a wet Atlantic environment. Its sugar accumulation can be strong, but the musts are not defined by the same unusually high acid retention associated with Petit Manseng.

The vineyard profile helps explain why Courbu often appears as part of a regional blend rather than as a universal single-varietal category.

## In the glass

Courbu can bring body, perfume, and a softer acid profile to white wines. Depending on harvest and élevage, the structural range can move from fresh and floral to richer, more honeyed or spicy territory.

In blends with Mansengs, the grape can broaden texture without erasing the region's natural freshness.

## Styles

### Dry whites

Dry still wine is the main contemporary reference, particularly in Basque and southwest-French blends.

### Sweet and late-harvest contexts

Courbu belongs historically to the same broader white-grape culture that produced concentrated sweet wines in the Pyrenean foothills, although it is less central today than Petit Manseng.

### Blends

This is perhaps the most important reference style. Courbu's value is often relational: how it changes the texture and aromatic shape of wines built with Mansengs and other local grapes.

## History & significance

Courbu is significant because it helps reveal how poorly national and appellation categories alone describe grape history. The same biological material can sit inside French Pyrenean wine and Spanish Basque wine under different legal and local names.

Its survival through the Txakoli revival also makes it a useful contrast with genuinely rare grapes such as Raffiat de Moncade.

## Names & genetics

**Prime CARTA identity:** Courbu.

Plantgrape states that Courbu is officially designated **Hondarrabi Zuri** in Spain. It also records Gros Courbu as an official propagation-material alternative in France.

This is the critical distinction:

- **Hondarrabi Zuri → Courbu**
- **Hondarrabi Zuri Zerratia → Petit Courbu** in current Bizkaiko law

Courbu and Petit Courbu remain separate biological entities. Courbu noir is another distinct identity and should not be collapsed into this page.

## Current developments

Courbu's current story is continuity more than rediscovery. Txakoli's contemporary visibility has kept Hondarrabi Zuri in active production, while CARTA's next work is to deepen the producer and wine anchors that demonstrate the grape's internal stylistic range.
