# Loire Valley

**A river-linked wine world that changes as it travels**

The Loire Valley is not one compact vineyard. Follow the Loire and its tributaries from the Atlantic-facing vineyards around Muscadet through Anjou-Saumur and Touraine toward Centre-Loire, and both the physical setting and the wine vocabulary change along the way.

## Read west to east

- **Nantais / Muscadet:** Atlantic influence and Melon de Bourgogne.
- **Anjou-Saumur:** Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Franc, dry and sweet still wines, and important sparkling traditions.
- **Touraine:** a broad middle zone where Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Franc, Sauvignon, Gamay, and other grapes divide across named origins such as Chinon and Vouvray.
- **Centre-Loire:** eastern Loire origins such as Sancerre sit outside a simplistic “one InterLoire region” story even while belonging to the larger Loire wine world.

The CARTA label is a derived orientation point built from the official mapped areas of selected child appellations. It is not a legal Loire Valley polygon.

## The measured picture—and its boundary

InterLoire’s official 2024 infographic reports 42,000 hectares for its own operating scope of 34 AOPs and one IGP. CARTA preserves that scope in the quantity record; it does not silently relabel the value as every vineyard in the Loire Valley. The same discipline applies to the official 2023 grape-share breakdown: Cabernet Franc, Chenin Blanc, Melon de Bourgogne, and Sauvignon are measured against the guide’s stated departmental denominator.

## Why the Loire matters

This is one of France’s clearest places to compare still and sparkling, dry and sweet, white, red and rosé wines without assuming that a single grape, soil, or cellar method explains the entire region. [Vouvray](../appellations/vouvray.md) makes the many lives of Chenin especially legible; Chinon foregrounds Cabernet Franc; Muscadet moves the learner into Melon de Bourgogne and Atlantic context.
