# Burgundy

**A long region read through nested origin**

Burgundy is less useful as one outline than as a hierarchy of named places. Regional appellations provide the broadest frame; village appellations and Premier Cru sites tighten origin; Grand Cru appellations occupy the narrowest tier. The official production split makes the hierarchy’s asymmetry visible: the most famous tier is also the smallest.

## A region without a fabricated polygon

CARTA does not draw an invented Burgundy wine-region boundary. Its Burgundy label is an orientation point derived from the official INAO shapes of governed child appellations, including Bourgogne, Bourgogne Côte d’Or, Bourgogne Aligoté, and Chablis Grand Cru. Select those shapes to see the legal areas that support the label.

## The physical spine

[Bourgogne Côte d’Or](../appellations/bourgogne-cote-d-or.md) is a geographic denomination inside Bourgogne AOC, not a synonym for every Côte d’Or wine. BIVB describes its north–south strip from Dijon to Maranges as roughly 65 kilometres long and generally one to two kilometres wide. That narrow spine is a practical geographic entry into the Côte de Nuits and Côte de Beaune, but it does not replace their many village and site identities.

## Grapes and scale

Official 2024 BIVB figures report 32,301 hectares and 84 appellations for Bourgogne. Chardonnay and Pinot Noir dominate the planted surface, with Aligoté a meaningful third grape. Those shares describe regional plantings; they do not override the rules of any selected appellation.

## Read the hierarchy carefully

BIVB’s five-year-average production breakdown reports 53% regional appellations, 46% village and Premier Cru appellations, and 1% Grand Cru appellations. These are production shares, not vineyard-area shares, price forecasts, or quality scores. CARTA keeps the measure and denominator attached to each number so the interface cannot blur those meanings.

## A current Human Reference route

[Numa Cornut](../../../producers/numa-cornut.md) and Bourgogne Aligoté provide one governed producer-and-grape path into the region. [Chablis Grand Cru](../appellations/chablis-grand-cru.md) provides another, geographically separate route. These anchors are intentionally selective and do not pretend to cover every major village or producer.
