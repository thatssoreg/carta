# Beaujolais

**One dominant grape, twelve AOCs, many distinct places**

Beaujolais lies immediately south of the Mâconnais and north of Lyon. Gamay overwhelmingly dominates the vineyard, but that shared grape should sharpen attention to origin rather than erase it. The regional frame contains the broad Beaujolais and Beaujolais-Villages contexts plus ten individually named crus in the north.

## How to read the map

The CARTA Beaujolais label is derived from mapped child-appellation geometry and is only an orientation anchor. Select [Morgon](../appellations/morgon.md), Fleurie, Moulin-à-Vent, or the broad Beaujolais AOC shape to compare official regulatory areas. The label itself is not a fabricated regional boundary.

## The hierarchy

The ten crus are Brouilly, Côte de Brouilly, Chénas, Chiroubles, Fleurie, Juliénas, Morgon, Moulin-à-Vent, Régnié, and Saint-Amour. They occupy the northern half of the region. Beaujolais and Beaujolais-Villages form the broader appellation context and can include red, white, and rosé wines.

## Grapes and colors

Official 2024 regional key figures report a 96% Gamay and 4% Chardonnay grape split, alongside a production-color split led by red wine. CARTA displays these as dated, scoped measures. They are not timeless definitions and they do not imply that every appellation has the same rules or color mix.

## A more complex ground story

Granite matters, especially in celebrated northern sites, but “Beaujolais equals granite” is too flat. The official regional terroir program used extensive borings and soil pits to identify hundreds of variants across complex geology. The responsible lesson is to investigate the named place, not predict taste from a regional slogan.
