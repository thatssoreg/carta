# Béarn

**Historical and cultural region in southwest France**

Béarn is a historical and cultural geography centered around Pau and the western Pyrenean foothills. In CARTA it is **not the same object as Béarn AOP**. That distinction matters because ordinary wine writing often uses “Béarn” loosely for a cultural landscape, a historical territory, and a legal wine name.

## Geography

Béarn sits between the high Pyrenees and lower Gascon/Adour landscapes. Atlantic influence remains strong, but altitude, slope, exposure, and distance from the mountains create significant internal variation.

The region's wine story is therefore better understood through foothills, valleys, soils, and appellations than through one homogeneous “Béarn terroir.”

## Wine geographies

The current CARTA slice emphasizes:

- [Jurançon](../appellations/jurancon.md), around the Pyrenean foothills near Pau
- [Pacherenc du Vic-Bilh](../appellations/pacherenc-du-vic-bilh.md), farther north/east in the Vic-Bilh landscape
- the legal Béarn appellation as a separate machine entity

For that separate legal appellation, INAO historical material records vineyard decline after nineteenth-century scale, VDQS recognition in 1951, and AOC recognition in 1975. Those dates belong to `appellation:bearn`; they do not turn the broader historical and cultural `place:bearn` into the appellation.

[Irouléguy](../appellations/irouleguy.md) belongs to French Basque Country and should not be casually absorbed into Béarn merely because all of these places share the same department.

## Grapes

Béarn's white-wine history is deeply connected to:

These grapes are more useful for understanding the region when treated as a network of materials and styles rather than as an appellation checklist.

## Styles

The region contains both dry and sweet white traditions. Jurançon and Pacherenc each have their own legal histories and style rules, but the shared capacity for late ripening, acid retention, and long autumns is a recurring theme.

## History & significance

Béarn's importance to CARTA is not one dramatic date. It is the persistence of a local grape culture through changing political, economic, and appellation systems.

The region also demonstrates why historical territory and modern wine law should remain separate in the ontology. A reader can care about Béarn as a cultural wine landscape without pretending every vineyard belongs to Béarn AOP.

## Producers to know

For the current white-wine slice:

- Domaine Cauhapé
- Clos Uroulat
- Domaine de Souch
- Camin Larredya
- Château Bouscassé in the Pacherenc/Vic-Bilh context

These are representative anchors, not a regional ranking.

## Current developments

Dry expressions, organic/biodynamic farming, renewed attention to local grapes, and small low-intervention producers are widening the external image of the area beyond sweet wine alone.
