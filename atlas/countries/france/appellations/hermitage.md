# Hermitage AOP

**One hill, three communes, and three ways of organising the same ground**

Hermitage was first recognised as an appellation d'origine by a judgment of the tribunal de Valence on 25 June 1936, then as an appellation d'origine contrôlée by the decree of 4 March 1937. It covers three Drôme communes on the left bank: Tain-l'Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage and Larnage. The specification in force is the one homologated by the arrêté of 10 December 2024.

## The commune trap

The commune named Crozes-Hermitage holds land in both Hermitage and [Crozes-Hermitage](crozes-hermitage.md). That is the cleanest possible demonstration that a commune name cannot tell a reader which wine a parcel may make: parcel delimitation decides, and CARTA never infers eligibility from municipal location.

## Red, white and a third form

Reds require [Syrah](../../../grapes/syrah.md) to be at least 85% of the encépagement with [Marsanne](../../../grapes/marsanne.md) and [Roussanne](../../../grapes/roussanne.md) as accessory varieties. Whites are Marsanne and Roussanne, and may additionally carry the [vin de paille mention](../../../classifications/hermitage-vin-de-paille.md): grapes dried at least 45 days, a minimum natural richness of 170 grams of sugar per litre, and a yield of 15 hectolitres per hectare.

## The hill is not the appellation

INAO describes the vineyard as sitting on a fragment of granitic massif separated from the Ardèche mountains by the Rhône, with south-facing terraced hillsides. The landform is one object; the appellation is another; the named sites growers talk about are a third, and CARTA draws none of them without surveyed or legal geometry.

## Three structures, not two styles

[Domaine Jean-Louis Chave](../../../producers/domaine-jean-louis-chave.md) builds one wine each year from several sites — "Bessards is our essential *climat*". [M. Chapoutier](../../../producers/m-chapoutier.md) has bottled *Sélections Parcellaires* since 1989 and converted to biodynamics from 1990. [Paul Jaboulet Aîné](../../../producers/paul-jaboulet-aine.md), founded on this hill in 1834, passed to the Frey family in 2006 without losing its house identity.

Multi-site blend, named-parcel selection, historic house under changed ownership: three ways of organising a hill, and none of them is "traditional versus modern".
