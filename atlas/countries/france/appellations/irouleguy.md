# Irouléguy

**French Basque Country, Pyrénées-Atlantiques, France**

Irouléguy is the principal appellation of French Basque wine country, centered on steep foothill vineyards around Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port and Saint-Étienne-de-Baïgorry. It produces dry still red, rosé, and white wines and sits at one of CARTA's most interesting boundaries: Basque culture, French wine law, Atlantic mountain geography, and nearby producers working just outside the appellation.

## Landscape

The vineyards occupy steep Pyrenean foothills in a humid Atlantic setting. Elevation, aspect, and slope are crucial because growers need sunlight and drainage in a wet climate.

The landscape is more useful than a generic “mountain wine” label. Valleys, ridges, airflow, and the relationship to the Atlantic create meaningful site differences that GIS should eventually expose.

## Grapes

White Irouléguy belongs to the same broader Pyrenean white-grape family as Jurançon and Pacherenc:

- [Petit Manseng](../../../grapes/petit-manseng.md)
- [Gros Manseng](../../../grapes/gros-manseng.md)
- [Courbu](../../../grapes/courbu.md)

Red and rosé wines introduce Tannat and other regional red material, making Irouléguy stylistically broader than the white-focused Pyrenean Atlantic pilot.

## Styles

### White

Dry white wines can combine Manseng acidity and ripeness with Courbu's body and perfume. The best examples can be textural and age-worthy without needing sweetness.

### Red and rosé

Irouléguy is also an important Tannat-based red and rosé appellation. CARTA's current white-grape focus should not obscure that.

Run 02 claimed sparkling wine was historically dominant and suggested broad pétillant legality. That claim did not survive normalization. Current INAO material describes the appellation's dry still red, rosé, and white wines.

## History & significance

Irouléguy matters because it gives French Basque wine a formal appellation identity without containing the entirety of Basque viticulture.

That distinction becomes concrete with [Imanol Garay](../../../producers/imanol-garay.md). Current trade reporting places some of his own vines at Saint-Étienne-de-Baïgorry **just outside** the appellation limits. The relationship is spatially precise in concept and still imprecise in geometry, which makes it a perfect GIS test.

## Producers to know

- **Domaine Arretxea** — a strong representative anchor for contemporary Irouléguy
- the local cooperative remains important to the appellation's production and history
- Imanol Garay is a useful **adjacent** producer, but CARTA does not call his reported outside-boundary vines Irouléguy

## Current developments

The region increasingly attracts attention for dry whites and small domaine production alongside its better-known reds. CARTA's next work should broaden its producer and wine anchors rather than letting one adjacent natural-wine network define the appellation.
