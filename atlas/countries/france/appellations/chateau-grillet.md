# Château-Grillet AOP

**Where appellation, estate, vineyard and wine almost become one thing**

Château-Grillet was recognised by the decree of 8 December 1936 — with [Saint-Péray](saint-peray.md), the oldest of the corridor's cru recognitions. It is a still white wine from [Viognier](../../../grapes/viognier.md) alone, over roughly 3.5 hectares in Saint-Michel-sur-Rhône and Vérin in the Loire department. The specification in force is the one homologated by the arrêté of 4 July 2024.

## Inside Condrieu, or beside it?

The Château-Grillet specification says the vineyard *a la particularité d'être inséré au cœur même de celui de l'appellation d'origine contrôlée « Condrieu »* — inserted into the heart of the Condrieu vineyard. The INAO product surface instead places it just south of [Condrieu](condrieu.md).

CARTA carries both statements and resolves neither. A description of a vineyard landscape and a delimited geographic area are different objects, and in neither reading does Château-Grillet become part of Condrieu: the two are independently delimited appellations, and physical continuity has never implied legal identity.

## Four identities, one owner

Artémis Domaines records the Pinault family acquiring the property in 2011 and describes it as one of the very few French monopole AOCs and one of the smallest. The specification's own historical account describes continuous single-family ownership from 1830 — a statement about the estate's past, not about who owns it now.

Even here, CARTA keeps [the appellation](chateau-grillet.md), [the estate](../../../producers/chateau-grillet-estate.md), the vineyard and the owning company as separate identities. One party controlling all four is a fact about control, not evidence that they are the same kind of thing.
