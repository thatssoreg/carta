# Bourgogne Côte d’Or

**A geographic denomination inside Bourgogne AOC**

Bourgogne Côte d’Or is a regional-appellation geographic denomination recognized in 2017. It covers a narrow corridor through 40 Côte de Nuits and Côte de Beaune villages from Dijon toward Maranges. It should not be confused with the Côte d’Or department, the whole Burgundy region, or the many village appellations encountered along the same slope.

## What the map shows

The shape is the current INAO cartographic representation of the denomination’s regulatory geographic area. It is not a map of planted vineyard parcels. On the wider CARTA map, it helps explain why Burgundy’s learner-facing anchor can be derived from official child geography without inventing a Burgundy polygon.

## Wine and scale

BIVB identifies Pinot Noir for red wine and Chardonnay for white wine. Its official page reports separate 2022 areas under production for red and white; CARTA stores them as separate, dated measurements rather than adding them into an unlabeled regional total.

## Why it matters

This denomination is a useful bridge between a broad regional label and village-level Burgundy. It names the Côte d’Or spine while remaining within the regional Bourgogne AOC tier. That makes it an especially clear example of how a geographic qualifier can add precision without becoming a village or Premier Cru appellation.
