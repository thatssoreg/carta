# The 1395 Gamay ordinance

**A historical regulatory event, not a modern migration map**

The Dijon municipal archive records Philip the Bold’s **1395 ordinance against Gamay**. CARTA treats it within the historical Duchy of Burgundy and uses later scholarship to contextualize ducal wine rules as instruments of vineyard, economic and political control.

## What is accepted

The ordinance targeted Gamay in ducal territory. It belongs in the history of agricultural regulation and the long relationship between Gamay and Burgundy.

## What is rejected

The event does not by itself document a clean, one-step migration from modern Burgundy into Beaujolais. CARTA therefore rejects the familiar one-sentence story that the grape was banned and simply “moved south.” Historical ducal territory must not be replaced by a modern Burgundy polygon.

The event also does not make Pinot and Gamay opposites in every later legal or viticultural context. Gamay’s genetic parentage—Pinot × Gouais blanc—is a separate biological claim.
