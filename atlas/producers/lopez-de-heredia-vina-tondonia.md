# R. López de Heredia Viña Tondonia

**Haro, Rioja Alta, Spain**

R. López de Heredia Viña Tondonia is one of Rioja's historic family bodegas and a particularly useful CARTA reference for the relationship among durable vineyard names, long élevage, legal classification, family continuity, and the persistent identity of a wine across vintages. **Viña Gravonia Blanco** is the seed that opens this world.

## Background & trajectory

The estate dates its beginning to **1877**, when Rafael López de Heredia y Landeta began planning and building the bodega in Haro after learning from French wine merchants working in Rioja. The producer's own history places the house firmly in Haro and presents its vineyards as long-lived named properties rather than anonymous regional sourcing.

CARTA currently models Rafael as the founder and the bodega as the durable producer identity. The 03D report supplied a much more elaborate family genealogy than the recovered first-party evidence supports, so that genealogy was not ingested.

## Haro and the vineyards

The bodega is based in **Haro**, within **Rioja Alta**. First-party material describes Viña Tondonia as planted in 1913 and 1914 and also names Viña Cubillo, Viña Bosconia, and Viña Zaconia among the estate's historic vineyards.

A current trade technical sheet describes Viña Gravonia as sourced from **Zaconia**, while the estate itself separately uses the Viña Zaconia name. That creates a real naming/identity question. CARTA has not yet created a Gravonia/Zaconia vineyard identity or synonym assertion because the exact crosswalk needs first-party confirmation.

## Viura

Recent Viña Gravonia technical sheets identify the wine as **100% Viura**. The Consejo Regulador DOCa Rioja states that Viura is also known as **Macabeo**, so CARTA represents Macabeo as a first-class synonym assertion on the Viura grape entity.

This is a naming relationship, not evidence for a second grape identity.

## Viña Gravonia Blanco

Viña Gravonia Blanco is represented as one durable wine identity across vintages. Recent technical sheets document:

- 100% Viura;
- fermentation in large oak vats;
- four years of oak aging;
- two rackings per year;
- fining with fresh egg whites;
- bottling without filtration.

These details are strong recent-vintage anchors. They should not be turned into a universal formula for every historical Gravonia vintage without evidence.

## Law and cellar tradition

The distinction between producer practice and Rioja law matters here. Run 03D incorrectly stated that egg-white fining was required by Rioja law and made other unsupported claims about minimum Viura percentages and release patterns. CARTA did not ingest those statements.

The house's unusually long élevage is therefore treated first as **López de Heredia practice**, with legal minimums to be added only from current regulatory authority in a later Rioja-focused pass.

## Style & significance

Gravonia is useful less because it needs a prestige claim than because it makes several parts of CARTA's architecture concrete. One wine can retain a stable identity while vintage, time in barrel, release timing, and other production facts vary. A historic vineyard name can also be culturally important while its exact machine identity remains unresolved.
