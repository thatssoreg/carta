# Clos du Tue-Boeuf

**Les Montils, Touraine — an estate, a work lineage, and wine-by-wine legal precision**

Clos du Tue-Boeuf is a Loire estate in Les Montils associated in the governed trade record with Thierry and Zoë Puzelat. That record distinguishes estate fruit from purchased fruit and records certified-organic farming. CARTA does not turn either point into a rule for Touraine or assume that every wine follows one sourcing or classification pattern.

## Why it is a Loire door

Tue-Boeuf opens several routes at once: Touraine’s middle-Loire diversity; the neighboring but distinct Cheverny and Cour-Cheverny legal origins; Menu Pineau and other secondary grapes; and Vin de France as a wine-level classification rather than an estate identity.

It also carries a documented work line. Pierre-Olivier Bonhomme worked seasons at the estate before becoming Thierry Puzelat’s partner in the Puzelat-Bonhomme négociant project and later continuing under his own name. This is an evidenced work/collaboration path, not a claim that every producer around it belongs to one movement.

## Le Brin de Chèvre, 2024

The represented 2024 Le Brin de Chèvre is a Vin de France white composed of 90% Menu Pineau and 10% Petit Meslier from neighboring parcels planted in 1937 and 1950, with later massale-selection replantings contributing part of the wine. Those dates do not support calling the vines pre-phylloxera.

Menu Pineau is connected to its supported local name, Orbois, through governed name authority. Petit Meslier remains a vintage-scoped component here rather than an automatic rule for the persistent wine identity.

## Law, map, and evidence boundary

Vin de France is a classification, not an appellation, physical container, or natural-wine badge. The Loire Valley, Touraine, Cheverny/Cour-Cheverny, and Les Montils remain separate geographic and legal records.

The Atlas marker is the official Les Montils municipality point. It is not a cellar coordinate, estate boundary, vineyard, or fruit-source map. Current management, ownership, and cellar roles remain a research gap where the evidence would otherwise turn co-management into an unsupported succession story.
