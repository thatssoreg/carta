# Goyo García Viadero

**Ribera del Duero and Liébana, Spain**

Goyo García Viadero is a Spanish grower-winemaker whose work connects a long Ribera del Duero family background to an independent producer identity and a small Cantabrian project in Liébana. CARTA keeps those layers separate enough to see what belongs to the person, the producer, the Beâtum label, and the individual wines.

## Background & trajectory

Trade reporting describes García Viadero as coming from a Ribera del Duero winemaking family and setting out independently in 2003. His main body of work remains associated with Ribera del Duero, but he also makes wines in Cantabria, where the **Beâtum** project becomes CARTA's current point of entry.

This baseline does not attempt to catalogue his entire Ribera portfolio.

## Beâtum

CARTA models **Beâtum** as a project/label within Goyo García Viadero's producer world, not as a separate estate.

The two current machine-authority anchors are:

- **Beâtum Blanco**
- **Beâtum Tinto**

Importer documentation places the fruit around Potes in **Valle de Liébana**, Picos de Europa, Cantabria.

## Place & vineyards

José Pastor Selections describes Beâtum Blanco as coming from old bush-vine Palomino on brown slate at roughly 500 to 600 meters around Potes.

Beâtum Tinto is documented from much older mixed plantings on broken slate. Exact parcels, tenure, and vineyard names remain unresolved, so CARTA does not invent a single estate vineyard geometry for the project.

## Grapes

### Palomino Fino

Beâtum Blanco is documented as **100% Palomino**.

Current accepted evidence identifies Beâtum Blanco as Palomino. CARTA does not attach Verdelho or Pedernã to this wine because the accepted source set does not support those identities.

### Mencía + Palomino

José Pastor documents Beâtum Tinto as approximately **80% Mencía and 20% Palomino**. Because blend composition can change, CARTA treats this as a documented production claim rather than an eternal formula for the wine.

## In the cellar

**Beâtum Blanco** is documented as fermenting on skins in stainless steel without added yeast, sulfur, or other exogenous winemaking products.

**Beâtum Tinto** is documented as a co-fermentation of Mencía and Palomino with wild yeasts in steel, followed by about 12 months in old French oak without added SO2.

These are current evidence-backed anchors, not a claim that every vintage follows exactly the same regime.

## Liébana context

The Government of Cantabria continues to maintain the legal and registration framework for **Vino de la Tierra de Liébana**. The official 2009 amendment lists **Palomino** among authorized white grapes and **Mencía** among authorized red grapes.

That matters because Beâtum's grape combination is not simply a stylistic oddity. Both varieties belong inside the legal grape vocabulary of the Liébana designation.

## Style & significance

Beâtum opens a different Palomino story from the grape's much more familiar southern Spanish associations. In CARTA, that does not become a claim that Liébana is “the new Jerez” or that one producer defines the region.

Instead, the project becomes a doorway into mountain viticulture, mixed red-and-white plantings, Cantabrian wine law, and Palomino's wider geography.
