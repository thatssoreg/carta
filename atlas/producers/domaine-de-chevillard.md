# Domaine de Chevillard

**Combe de Savoie, France**

Domaine de Chevillard is a small Savoie estate led by Matthieu Goury, with Guillemette Renard materially involved in vineyard work and plant-based preparations. The domaine is a strong entry point into the Combe de Savoie, Jacquère, Altesse, and the legal geography of names such as Apremont.

## Background & trajectory

Current first-party material presents Goury and Renard as the people behind the estate. CARTA keeps them as person identities alongside the producer rather than turning the estate into a biography of one individual.

The domaine's own current pages disagree slightly on scale: one describes approximately 10 hectares, another approximately 12. CARTA therefore records the estate as roughly 10 to 12 hectares and leaves exact current acreage provisional.

## Place & vineyards

The domaine works in the **Combe de Savoie**, with core vineyard presence around Saint-Pierre-d'Albigny and Saint-Jean-de-la-Porte. This is Alpine wine country where municipality, cru name, appellation, slope, and geological history overlap without being interchangeable.

Parcel geometry has not yet been ingested. The famous Mont Granier landslide is a high-value future landscape research question, but the 03D report relied too heavily on secondary/tourism material for some of its detailed geological claims, so CARTA did not overstate them here.

## Farming

The domaine states that it entered organic conversion in 2017 and biodynamic conversion in 2020. Guillemette Renard is described as preparing plant-based vineyard treatments.

Run 03D made stronger claims about using no copper or sulfur and about unusually severe bunch thinning. Those claims were not supported strongly enough by the recovered first-party material and were not admitted as authority.

## Grapes

**Jacquère** is central to the domaine's Savoie white-wine work. **Altesse** is the grape behind its Roussette de Savoie bottling. Mondeuse is also part of the estate's local-grape vocabulary.

CARTA does not treat Jacquère and Gringet as synonyms. The research report blurred that distinction, and the claim did not survive reconciliation.

## Apremont and appellation context

The domaine lists an **Apremont** cru within its AOP Savoie range. INAO publishes **Vin de Savoie Apremont** as an AOP product surface within the broader Savoie / Vin de Savoie framework. CARTA therefore represents `appellation:vin-de-savoie-apremont` as a governed legal-geographic surface nested under `appellation:vin-de-savoie`, rather than pretending Apremont is unrelated to the broader appellation.

This is intentionally more precise than the 03D report's inconsistent claim that Apremont is simultaneously not an appellation and yet should be treated like one.

## In the cellar

The domaine describes indigenous-yeast vinification, restrained use of wood, and a release rhythm in which wines spend roughly a year in barrel and another year in bottle before sale.
