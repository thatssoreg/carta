# Condado de Haza / Alenza Gran Reserva

**Roa / Ribera del Duero, Spain**

The cellar seed **Alenza Gran Reserva** resolves to **Condado de Haza**, one of the wineries in the current Familia Fernández Rivera portfolio. This is a major correction to Run 03G, which incorrectly assigned Alenza to Finca Villacreces.

## Family, company and estate

Familia Fernández Rivera traces its wine enterprise to Alejandro Fernández and Esperanza Rivera and currently presents a four-winery portfolio: Tinto Pesquera, Condado de Haza, Dehesa La Granja and El Vínculo. The family says its granddaughters took charge of the group in 2019.

Condado de Haza is in Roa, Burgos. Its current first-party profile describes a 200-hectare vineyard estate and a commitment to 100% organic production.

## Alenza

Alenza is one durable named wine identity across vintages.

For **2019**, the producer documents:

- winery: Condado de Haza;
- grape: 100% Tempranillo;
- vineyard: Maricalva;
- source geography: the municipal areas of Roa and La Horra at roughly 800 m;
- soil: clay with sand and gravel in varying proportions;
- élevage: 27 months in American oak;
- stem use from Maricalva fruit as part of the wine's identity.

Those are 2019-scoped facts unless another source establishes continuity.

The wine's name is a tribute to **Alejandro Fernández and Esperanza Rivera**.

## Ribera del Duero naming and law

The Ribera del Duero Consejo Regulador identifies **Tempranillo**, **Tinto Fino** and **Tinta del País** as regional names for the same principal grape. CARTA therefore reuses `grape:tempranillo` and records Tinto Fino and Tinta del País as Ribera-scoped local names.

Current Ribera del Duero specifications require red **Gran Reserva** to age at least 60 months in total, including at least 24 months in oak barrels of no more than 330 L, with the remainder in bottle.

CARTA keeps that legal requirement separate from Condado de Haza's vintage-specific 27-month American-oak practice.
