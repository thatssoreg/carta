# Château Simone

**Meyreuil, Palette, France**

Château Simone is the Rougier family's historic estate in Meyreuil and one of the central reference points for the tiny Palette appellation. The seed wine, **Château Simone Blanc**, opens a world of Clairette-led white wine, old mixed vineyard material, north-facing Provençal slopes, and an unusually close relationship between one estate and an appellation's modern history.

## History & family

Château Simone states that the property has been in the **Rougier family since 1830**. Current CARTA authority includes Jean-François Rougier as a contemporary person in the estate story, but does not yet attempt a complete seven-generation succession graph.

INAO independently describes Palette's history as closely tied to Château Simone and notes that the Rougier family pursued the origin designation obtained in 1948 for a broader collection of nearby hamlets and estates. That supports a major historical role without requiring the stronger claim that Château Simone single-handedly created the appellation.

## Place & terroir

The estate is in **Meyreuil**, about four kilometres from Aix-en-Provence. First-party material places the vineyard on the **north-facing slopes of the Massif du Montaiguet**, in a protected bowl crossed by the Arc river, with limestone scree, clay, pebbles, and gravel.

This corrects a significant Run 03D error. The report repeatedly placed the vineyards on Mont Sainte-Victoire. Sainte-Victoire is part of the wider visual and regional landscape, but the estate's own terroir description anchors Château Simone on the Montaiguet.

## Vineyard & farming

The producer says the vineyard was rebuilt after phylloxera and that some parcels are now more than 125 years old. The soils are cultivated by tillage without weedkillers or chemical fertilizers.

CARTA records these specific practices. It does not turn them into an unsupported claim of continuous organic certification since the nineteenth century.

## Palette

INAO defines the **Palette AOP** production area across Aix-en-Provence, Meyreuil, and Le Tholonet in the Arc valley/cirque. For white wine, Clairette must be the majority variety.

Palette and Château Simone are therefore deeply related but remain separate machine identities: one is a legal-geographic appellation, the other a producer.

## Château Simone Blanc

The current producer technical sheet gives a precise blend:

- **80% Clairette**
- **10% Grenache Blanc**
- **5% Bourboulenc**
- **3% Ugni Blanc**
- **2% Muscat Blanc**

The wine is hand harvested, fermented with native yeasts, and matured in small oak for 18 months, including six months on fine lees, before bottling after a year in barrel.

These percentages are current technical authority, not a claim that every historical vintage followed the exact same formula.

Run 03D also asserted that malolactic fermentation is categorically suppressed. The recovered first-party technical page did not support that strongly enough, so CARTA did not ingest it.

## Grapes

Clairette is the structural center of the white wine and of Palette's legal white-wine identity. Grenache Blanc, Bourboulenc, Ugni Blanc, and Muscat Blanc are meaningful supporting grapes in the current Château Simone Blanc formula.

The report's discussion of Téoulier/Manosquin and other minor grapes belongs primarily to the broader Palette grape set and historical research, not to the current white blend, so those identities were not forced into this ingestion.
