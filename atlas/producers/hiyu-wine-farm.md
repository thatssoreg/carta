# Hiyu Wine Farm

**Hood River, Oregon, United States**

Hiyu Wine Farm is a Hood River producer whose estate wines, Tzum site wines, farming system, and long élevage make it a useful reference for understanding the Columbia Gorge as more than a single regional style. CARTA keeps the home farm, the Tzum project, and individual sites distinct so that the relationships remain legible.

## Background & trajectory

Nate Ready and China Tresemer are documented co-founders of Hiyu. Importer reporting describes Ready's earlier restaurant work, including The French Laundry, and an apprenticeship with Maggie Harrison at Antica Terra. CARTA records those work and training relationships without turning them into a broad claim that Hiyu is simply an extension of another producer's style.

## Place & vineyards

Hiyu is based in Hood River, Oregon, within the Columbia Gorge wine world. The producer's own site distinguishes wines from named fields on the home farm from **Tzum**, which comes from small, specific fields throughout the Gorge.

**Moon Hill Farm** is the most fully resolved Tzum site in CARTA so far. Importer documentation describes it as a two-acre vineyard directly adjacent to Hiyu, once part of the same farm, planted in 2010 and cared for by the Hiyu team since 2015 under a long-term lease.

Exact parcel boundaries, slope, aspect, and land ownership remain GIS and source-resolution questions.

## Farming

Hiyu's own materials present the farm as a deliberately integrated landscape of vines, animals, gardens, forest, compost, and cellar work. Importer reporting describes organic and regenerative practice, agroforestry, dry farming, and unusually low-input vineyard management.

CARTA treats these descriptions as producer-specific practice, not as a template for what all Columbia Gorge or natural wine farming should look like.

## In the cellar

Hiyu describes hand work, whole clusters, wild fermentation, and long maturation as recurring parts of the cellar. The important point is variation rather than one fixed recipe: direct pressing, pre-fermentation maceration, extended skin contact, neutral wood, and long élevage can all appear depending on field and wine.

### Moon Hill as a time problem

Tzum Moon Hill Farm is especially useful because it tests CARTA's decision not to create vintage-specific wine identities. The wine has one durable identity, while its production history changes through time.

Importer documentation describes wines from the 2015 through 2020 vintages being vinified and aged separately, then assembled in spring 2021 in 500-liter puncheons with the aim of maintaining a solera-like system. CARTA therefore stores the multi-vintage history as dated claims attached to the wine rather than creating a different entity for each component vintage or consumer-facing release number.

## Grapes

Moon Hill is documented with:

- **Chardonnay** as the dominant variety, including an heirloom California Wente selection and cuttings from Celilo Vineyard
- **Pinot Gris**
- **Pinot Noir**

CARTA does not equate the Wente material with Dijon clone 96. The current evidence does not support that identification.

## Wines & projects to know

**Tzum** is Hiyu's site-specific project/label for small fields beyond the home-farm Hiyu bottlings.

**Tzum Moon Hill Farm / Columbia Gorge White** is the strongest current CARTA anchor. It is documented as roughly 80% Chardonnay with Pinot Gris and Pinot Noir making up the balance, and its multi-vintage assemblage makes it particularly useful for studying time, site, and persistent wine identity.

The wider Hiyu and Tzum ranges are substantially larger than this baseline. CARTA has not turned every named field into an entity merely because a portfolio list exists.

## Style & significance

Hiyu is valuable to CARTA because several systems meet here at once: a highly specific farm, a network of nearby leased sites, field blends, long élevage, and wines whose identity can persist even when their vintage composition changes.

That complexity is more useful than collapsing Hiyu into a generic “natural Oregon” category.

## Appellation context

The TTB recognizes **Columbia Gorge** as an American Viticultural Area. CARTA has not yet ingested official polygon geometry, so Moon Hill's placement is currently source-described rather than parcel-mapped.
