# Domaine Alain Voge

**Saint-Péray and Cornas · one estate holding both of an appellation's wine forms**

Domaine Alain Voge is the clearest answer to the assumption that the Northern Rhône is red Syrah country. It works [Cornas](../countries/france/appellations/cornas.md) and [Saint-Péray](../countries/france/appellations/saint-peray.md), two appellations that nearly touch and share a granite basement while agreeing on almost nothing else.

## The sparkling line

Les Bulles d'Alain is 100% [Marsanne](../grapes/marsanne.md), méthode traditionnelle, brut nature, with a [second fermentation in bottle](../practices/traditional-method-sparkling.md) and four years on lattes. The estate presents it as perpetuating the traditional-method Saint-Péray that made the region's reputation from the 1830s — the tradition whose beginning the specification traces to [Louis-Alexandre Faure's 1828 trials](../historical-events/saint-peray-sparkling-1828.md).

## The still line, and what 1984 actually changed

The Ongrie cuvée was created in 1984: 100% Marsanne, vinified exclusively in 228- and 400-litre oak. The estate's own page explains the context — at that time *l'essentiel de la production de Saint-Péray était issue de la méthode traditionnelle et les vins étaient vinifiés en cuves*: most Saint-Péray was sparkling, and still wines were made in tank.

So Alain Voge is recorded here as the precursor of barrel vinification and élevage in Saint-Péray. He is not recorded as the inventor of still Saint-Péray, which the same passage says already existed. Innovation inside a tradition is a more interesting claim than invention, and it is the one the evidence supports.
