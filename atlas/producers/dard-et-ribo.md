# Dard & Ribo

**Crozes-Hermitage, Hermitage and Saint-Joseph · rented land, whole bunches, and an argument with the word "natural"**

Dard & Ribo are a genuine node in the Northern Rhône's low-sulfur history, and the most useful thing about them is that their own testimony complicates the category they are usually filed under.

## Two founding dates, both true

René-Jean Dard's account gives 1983 as the first vintage he and François Ribo made together, and 1984 as the year they "began properly". CARTA keeps both, because they are two different events rather than one contested date.

## A patchwork, not an estate

Dard describes the holding being assembled by renting vineyards from their owners and expanding gradually across parcels in [Crozes-Hermitage](../countries/france/appellations/crozes-hermitage.md), [Hermitage](../countries/france/appellations/hermitage.md) and [Saint-Joseph](../countries/france/appellations/saint-joseph.md). Rented land is not owned land, and one cellar point at Mercurol could not stand for that graph even if a coordinate had been acquired. None has.

## Practice, stated precisely

From the mid-1980s they decided to plough rather than use herbicides. On certification, Dard is direct: what interests them is "the notion of working cleanly" rather than holding a logo for the label — an absence of certification that is a stated choice, not evidence of conventional farming.

Reds get ten to fifteen days of maceration with [whole bunches](../practices/carbonic-maceration.md) — unless sunburn requires sorting, in which case a destemmer is used — fermented in large wood with the cap broken once a day and aged in 500-litre demi-muids. Whites are pressed whole-bunch.

## The sulfur position

Dard told Louis/Dressner that making natural wine "should not be a goal unto itself" but "a tool to make qualitatively superior wine", and that he had made sulfur-free wines since he was fifteen because his father never sulfured. He told LITTLEWINE that their goal is not to make wine without sulfites but to make good wine, and that if a wine is going a bit wild they do not stop themselves adding some.

CARTA records a deep low-sulfur history and an explicit refusal of zero-sulfur absolutism. It encodes neither as a category, and it does not classify the producer into a movement on the strength of cellar practices.
