# Caparzo

**Montalcino, Italy**

Caparzo is a Montalcino estate established in 1970. The cellar seed **Brunello di Montalcino Vigna La Casa** opens a world around Sangiovese/Brunello naming, vineyard-designated Brunello, Montosoli, producer practice versus DOCG law, and the history of separately vinified sites in Montalcino.

## Producer and people

The Brunello di Montalcino Consorzio states that Caparzo was established in 1970 and purchased by Elisabetta Gnudi Angelini in 1998.

Current producer material identifies Massimo Bracalente as winemaker/oenologist.

CARTA records those specific roles without extending them into unsupported succession or influence relationships.

## Vigna La Casa

Caparzo's first-party vineyard description gives La Casa as:

- 5 hectares of Brunello vines;
- 275 metres elevation;
- south-to-southeast exposure;
- schist-clay soil locally described as galestro.

This resolves a major internal contradiction in Run 03E, which gave La Casa elevations of roughly 220–250 metres in one section and 500–550 metres in another.

Caparzo connects the La Casa property and surrounding Vigna La Casa wine context to Montosoli hill. CARTA keeps that hill relationship provisional until more precise spatial authority is acquired. No Montosoli polygon is invented.

## Vigna La Casa as wine

Caparzo identifies 1977 as the first vintage of **Brunello di Montalcino Vigna La Casa**.

The Consorzio says that in 1977 Caparzo introduced the cru concept to Montalcino by vinifying the grapes of La Casa separately. CARTA preserves that carefully scoped historical claim. It does not inflate it into a claim that Caparzo made Italy's first single-vineyard wine.

## Sangiovese and Brunello

The Consorzio's current production rules state that Brunello di Montalcino is made from **Sangiovese**, also called **Brunello** in Montalcino.

CARTA therefore creates one Sangiovese grape identity with a jurisdiction-bound local-name assertion for Brunello. “Brunello” is not treated as a second grape.

## Law versus producer practice

The current Consorzio rules state a minimum of two years in oak plus four months in bottle for Brunello di Montalcino.

That corrects Run 03E's claim that DOCG law requires 36 months in oak.

Caparzo's current Vigna La Casa protocol is a separate producer practice: the estate describes 12 months in French oak barriques followed by 24 months in 30-hectolitre French oak barrels. CARTA stores that as current producer practice, not as Brunello law and not as an assumption about every historical vintage.

## Spatial status

The vineyard is source-described in Montalcino at 275 metres with south-to-southeast exposure. The Montosoli relationship remains provisional and no parcel geometry is stored.
