# Villalobos

**Lolol, Colchagua Valley, Chile**

Villalobos is a family wine project in Lolol in Chile's Colchagua Valley. Its defining CARTA anchor is **Viñedo Silvestre**, an old Carignan vineyard that the family describes as having grown wild for decades among native vegetation with no pruning or irrigation. The seed opens a Chilean world centered on dry farming, old Carignan, family stewardship, and the practical support of French winemaker Mathieu Rousseau.

## Family & beginnings

Trade documentation identifies **Enrique Villalobos** and **Rita Encina** among the family principals. In a 2015 interview, the family says it began making a small quantity of wine in **2007** with support from their friend **Mathieu Rousseau**.

CARTA records that as a documented collaboration/support relationship. It does not elevate Rousseau into an unsupported formal mentor, employee, or founder role.

## Viñedo Silvestre

The producer describes Viñedo Silvestre as an approximately **80-year-old Carignan vineyard** in Lolol. The vines were left wild for decades and grow amid native Chilean vegetation. The source describes no pruning and no irrigation.

The vineyard is placed about **30 kilometres from the Pacific Ocean and around 100 metres above sea level**. Run 03D reported roughly 300 metres, which did not survive first-party verification.

CARTA does not describe these vines as `pre-phylloxera` or as having `survived phylloxera`. Their age and own-rooted status can be interesting without importing a European phylloxera narrative that the evidence does not support.

## Viñedo Silvestre Carignan

The corresponding wine is represented as one durable **Viñedo Silvestre Carignan** identity across vintages. Villalobos documents it as:

- 100% Carignan;
- hand harvested;
- spontaneously fermented with native yeast in stainless steel;
- unpressed;
- unfined and unfiltered;
- settled by gravity;
- moved through spontaneous malolactic fermentation;
- aged for 18 months in old neutral French oak.

This is one of the cleaner places where the vineyard and wine need separate CARTA identities. The vineyard is a physical agricultural site; the wine is a repeatable named cuvée made from its fruit.

## Place

Lolol sits within the Colchagua Valley wine context. CARTA currently uses source-described municipality and regional placement rather than inventing a more specific legal indication for the wine.

Run 03D floated `Vino de la Tierra de Pelarco`, coastal IG language, and possible VIGNO membership. None was strong enough to ingest. VIGNO and Chilean Carignan law should be researched as their own authority questions rather than inferred from grape or style.

## Farming & significance

What makes Silvestre useful is not a need to call it the world's most unique vineyard. The evidence is already compelling at a narrower level: old Carignan, long unmanaged growth, dry farming, native vegetation, and a family decision to keep the vineyard's unusual structure intact.

CARTA avoids turning those facts into universal claims about Chilean Carignan or natural wine.
