# Alfredo Egia

**Balmaseda, Bizkaia, Spain**

Alfredo Egia is a Basque grower and winemaker working around Balmaseda, in the inland part of Bizkaia. His wines are useful references for a broader contemporary Txakoli conversation: local and Pyrenean grapes, Atlantic growing conditions, serious texture, and cellar choices that can push well beyond the light, immediately brisk stereotype often attached to the category.

## Background & trajectory

Trade and specialist reporting describe Egia as trained in viticulture and enology, with experience outside Bizkaia before returning to build his own work. Imanol Garay is a documented friend, collaborator, and mentor. That relationship matters because it connects Egia to a wider low-intervention network without requiring CARTA to invent a direct apprenticeship to Richard Leroy.

## Place & vineyards

Egia is based around **Balmaseda**, at the western edge of Bizkaia. Spanish Wine Lover described his Egia Enea vineyard as two south-facing hectares around 225 metres, divided into three plots planted in 2001. More recent trade material describes a broader farming footprint, so CARTA treats the older acreage as a time-bound vineyard description rather than a permanent estate total.

Parcel geometry, tenure, slope, aspect, and geology are the next level of work. The important point for now is that Egia belongs to an inland Bizkaian context rather than a generic coastal-Txakoli picture.

## Farming

Current reporting describes organic and biodynamic-oriented farming, with close attention to soil life and low-input viticulture. CARTA records these practices at the level the evidence supports and avoids turning one interview or vintage into an eternal farming recipe.

## In the cellar

Egia's range is built around spontaneous fermentation, lees contact, and vessels that can include barrel and amphora. **Rebel Rebel** is the best-documented current technical example, but it should not stand in for every wine. Sulfur use is treated as wine- and vintage-specific rather than summarized as a universal “zero sulfur” rule.

The recurring impression is not a single technique. It is a willingness to use time, oxygen, lees, vessel, and local grapes to build broader, more textural whites than the most familiar Txakoli model.

## Wines to know

**Egia Enea** is a useful place-driven anchor for the estate and its Balmaseda vineyard story.

**Lexardi** broadens the reference beyond a single flagship and helps show that Egia's work is a range, not one bottle.

**Rebel Rebel** is the best-documented CARTA wine so far. It links Petit Courbu and Petit Manseng to a more textural, oxidative-leaning, lees-shaped interpretation of Bizkaian white wine.

**Izaki** is another useful reference for Egia's work with the local white-grape set.

These are representative anchors, not an exhaustive catalogue.

## Style & significance

Egia is most useful as a reference for what happens when Txakoli's local grapes are treated as material for serious, ageable, textural wine rather than as a mandate for one familiar style. His work expands the reader's frame without requiring the false claim that he represents all of Bizkaia or that low-intervention wine is inherently more authentic.

## Appellation context

Egia's vineyards and grape choices sit inside the [Bizkaiko Txakolina](../countries/spain/appellations/bizkaiko-txakolina.md) conversation.
