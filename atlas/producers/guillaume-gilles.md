# Guillaume Gilles / Les Peyrouses

**Cornas, Northern Rhône, France**

The cellar seed **Les Peyrouses** is a useful STRATA case because physical place and legal wine origin diverge.

The red Les Peyrouses comes from an old-vine Syrah parcel **on the flats in the commune of Cornas**, but the wine is bottled as **Côtes du Rhône Rouge**, not Cornas AOP.

## Guillaume Gilles

Specialist sources place Guillaume Gilles in Cornas and document his independent domaine from 2007.

His relationship to **Robert Michel is not speculative**. Rosenthal explicitly calls Gilles Michel's protégé and says Michel mentored him, leased him a prime Chaillot parcel and provided his cave. Vine Trail gives the chronology: Michel offered Gilles an apprenticeship in 2000; Gilles worked with him through Michel's 2006 retirement, then spent time with **Jean-Louis Chave** in Hermitage before striking out on his own.

Vine Trail also identifies the respected Cornas vigneron **Marcel Juge** as Gilles's grandfather.

## Les Peyrouses Rouge

Rosenthal identifies **Les Peyrouses Côtes du Rhône Rouge** as:

- Syrah;
- from an old-vines parcel on the flats in Cornas;
- sand and clay soils overlain with large galet stones;
- vinified with the same approach used for Gilles's more prestigious cellar wines.

CARTA creates separate identities for the **Les Peyrouses vineyard** and the **Les Peyrouses Rouge wine** because Gilles also makes a white wine from the named site.

## Cornas versus Côtes du Rhône

INAO defines Cornas AOP as a red-wine appellation delimited on the sole commune of Cornas, with Syrah as the sole grape.

That does **not** mean every vineyard physically located in the commune qualifies for Cornas AOP. The Les Peyrouses parcel is described as physically in Cornas, while the red wine is presented as **Côtes du Rhône**.

This is exactly the kind of legal/spatial distinction CARTA is designed to preserve.

## Farming and cellar

Vine Trail reports certified-organic farming from 2020. Its current producer profile describes whole-bunch red fermentation, native yeasts, no fining or filtration, older wood and restrained sulfur use.

Those are producer-level current practices. They are not automatically universalized into exact Les Peyrouses percentages or durations without wine-specific evidence.
