# Co-fermentation and grape-stage assemblage

**A rule about when varieties may meet — not a description of what anyone did**

Four Northern Rhône specifications — [Côte-Rôtie](../countries/france/appellations/cote-rotie.md), [Saint-Joseph](../countries/france/appellations/saint-joseph.md), [Crozes-Hermitage](../countries/france/appellations/crozes-hermitage.md) and [Hermitage](../countries/france/appellations/hermitage.md) — require that wines made from both the red and the white permitted varieties be *vinifiés par assemblage des raisins concernés*: assembled as grapes, in the same proportion, rather than blended after fermentation.

## Why the distinction matters

Blending two finished wines and fermenting two varieties together are different operations with different results, and the specifications govern only the second. In Côte-Rôtie that is the rule behind the corridor's most quoted fact — that [Viognier](../grapes/viognier.md) may join [Syrah](../grapes/syrah.md) in a red wine, up to 20% of the vines.

## What the rule does not say

It does not say that any producer uses the white variety, that a wine using it approaches the maximum, or that a given vintage was handled that way. Many Côte-Rôtie wines contain no Viognier at all. CARTA records the legal permission on the appellation and any actual practice on the producer or the wine, and never lets one stand in for the other.

Read the practice through [Northern Rhône](../countries/france/regions/northern-rhone.md).
