# Carbonic, semi-carbonic, and whole-cluster fermentation

These terms overlap in some cellars, but they are not synonyms.

## Carbonic maceration

Full carbonic maceration begins with intact whole bunches in a closed vessel whose atmosphere excludes oxygen with carbon dioxide. Intracellular metabolism begins inside intact berries before pressing and completion of alcoholic fermentation.

## Semi-carbonic maceration

In semi-carbonic maceration, juice released in a whole-bunch vat begins yeast fermentation and generates carbon dioxide around intact berries above. Ordinary fermentation and some intracellular metabolism proceed together without requiring a fully established external-CO₂ treatment.

## Whole-cluster fermentation

Whole-cluster describes the inclusion of intact bunches and stems. It does not by itself tell a reader whether the ferment is carbonic, semi-carbonic or neither. Native fermentation is another independent question concerning microbial management.

## Beaujolais is not one protocol

The August 2026 Beaujolais specification describes a regional tradition inspired by semi-carbonic maceration in its explanatory causal-link text. It does not impose carbonic or semi-carbonic maceration on every qualifying wine.

Domaine Marcel Lapierre currently describes semi-carbonic work. Domaine de la Grand’Cour describes whole bunches in CO₂-saturated tanks. These are producer claims, not legal definitions for the region.
